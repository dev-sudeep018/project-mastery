# ZeroShot gamification: evidence and implementation plan

Research date: 31 October 2026. This is a product design recommendation, not a claim that gamification has already improved ZeroShot's learning outcomes. ZeroShot was previously named Project Mastery.

## Decision

Build a personal learning journey: choose a useful next step, work through a real operation, receive specific test feedback, see durable progress, and choose what to do next. Add optional practice XP, attainable milestones, and a flexible weekly goal around that loop. Keep lessons, hints, explanations, and projects accessible regardless of points or pace.

The intended outcome is more sustained, successful learning. Session duration and number of button presses are poor substitutes. A passing submission records checked work; it cannot establish unaided understanding or long-term mastery by itself.

## What the evidence supports

| Evidence                                                                                                                                                                        | Finding and limits                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                        | Design implication for ZeroShot                                                                                                                                                     |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| [Ryan and Deci, 2000](https://www.selfdeterminationtheory.org/SDT/documents/2000_RyanDeci_SDT.pdf), self-determination theory                                                   | Motivation depends partly on support for competence, autonomy, and relatedness. This is a theoretical and empirical synthesis, not a test of our interface.                                                                                                                                                               | Provide useful feedback, meaningful choice, and an optional pace. Social features should support actual connection when available.                                                  |
| [Sailer et al., 2017](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2016.12.033), experimental study                                                                                            | Groups of game elements affected different psychological needs in an order-picking task. Badges, leaderboards, and performance graphs supported competence; the results do not isolate every individual element or establish effects in coding education.                                                                 | Select mechanics for a specific purpose. A badge should explain an accomplishment; adding a trophy icon alone is not a motivational strategy.                                       |
| [Sailer and Homner, 2020](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09498-w), meta-analysis                                                                          | Average effects favored gamification for cognitive, motivational, and behavioral outcomes (Hedges' g .49, .36, .25 respectively). Effects varied, and motivation/behavior findings were less stable in the higher-rigor subset.                                                                                           | Gamification is worth evaluating, but no universal retention uplift or guaranteed change in motivation should be promised.                                                          |
| [Hanus and Fox, 2015](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0360131514002000), classroom comparison                                                                | Across a 16-week semester, 71 students in two courses experienced either a badge/leaderboard design or the comparison curriculum. The gamified course had poorer motivation-related outcomes and final exam performance. This bundled, course-level comparison does not prove that all badges or leaderboards cause harm. | Avoid imposing public comparison and rewards as the main reason to learn. Allow a quiet experience, and measure learning as well as participation.                                  |
| [Mekler et al., 2017](https://edoc.unibas.ch/entities/publication/ccec8ccc-aa54-417d-b205-334b518d6eed), image-annotation experiment                                            | Points, levels, and leaderboards increased output quantity without significant improvement in competence or intrinsic motivation in this task.                                                                                                                                                                            | Do not treat more submissions as more learning. Award credit once for a meaningful outcome, not on every run.                                                                       |
| [van den Broek et al., 2026](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/gamified-feedback-in-adaptive-retrieval-practice-points-and-progr/), vocabulary retrieval experiment | In 166 adults, points with or without progress bars improved several self-reported motivational measures. Learning behavior and delayed recall did not improve. The task was vocabulary practice, not programming.                                                                                                        | Progress feedback may make effort feel better without improving retention. Keep those claims separate and preserve the instructional work.                                          |
| [Roediger and Karpicke, 2006](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/), prose-learning experiments                                                                            | Retrieval practice improved delayed retention relative to repeated study, despite the latter producing greater confidence.                                                                                                                                                                                                | Later review should ask learners to reconstruct or explain an idea, not simply reopen a completed page. Applying this result to coding is a design inference that needs evaluation. |
| [Cepeda et al., 2008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19076480/), spaced-learning experiment                                                                                    | The useful gap between learning sessions depended on the intended retention interval. There is no single optimal schedule for every case.                                                                                                                                                                                 | Offer spaced review later, with an explicit heuristic schedule; do not market fixed 1/3/7-day intervals as a scientifically optimal coding curriculum.                              |
| [Lally et al., 2010](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674), everyday habit study                                                                                                    | Habit development varied substantially; missing one opportunity did not materially derail the modeled process. Participants practiced self-chosen eating, drinking, or activity behaviors, not coding.                                                                                                                    | Flexible weekly pacing is a reasonable product choice. Avoid implying that missing a day destroys learning or that everybody forms a habit in a fixed number of days.               |

These sources justify testing a supportive design, not copying an assumed universal formula. Exact XP amounts, thresholds, badge names, and weekly targets below are product decisions, not research findings.

## First implementation

| Mechanic             | Recommended form                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Why it belongs                                                                                                                                                                               |
| -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Personal practice XP | 10 XP for each distinct published lesson whose current-version complete test suite has passed; 30 XP for the checked retrieval pipeline. The same lesson amount at every difficulty. Show both rules beside the score.                                               | Gives clear, predictable feedback while valuing substantive beginner work. The larger project amount recognizes integration work; the exact 10/30 amounts are a product choice.              |
| Practice level       | One level per 100 XP, starting at level 1. Show the next threshold and explain that levels count checked practice.                                                                                                                                                   | Breaks a large catalog into smaller milestones. Do not use labels such as expert or certified engineer.                                                                                      |
| Weekly goal          | Off by default; choices of 2, 4, or 6 new checked lessons in a Monday–Sunday local week. Let learners change or disable it at any time.                                                                                                                              | Supports choice without making a daily obligation. Show actual count even above the target; cap the visual progress bar. No lost XP or punishment after an unfinished week.                  |
| Learning quests      | One recommended next lesson in a chosen beginner track or domain path, with its purpose and prerequisites. Also offer browse freely.                                                                                                                                 | Turns progress into a concrete next action, with user control. A quest is a normal lesson, not a duplicate content item.                                                                     |
| Skill milestones     | First checked lesson; one checked lesson in each domain; each of the three six-lesson beginner tracks; checked retrieval pipeline. Show the next ten-lesson milestone alongside levels. Give each achievement an explicit criterion and a link to the relevant work. | Records identifiable accomplishments. Track badges are more informative than an undifferentiated total score. A pipeline badge is not a claim that the whole open-ended project is finished. |
| Completion feedback  | Inline success message after the first full-suite pass: what passed, XP if enabled, any newly reached milestone, and one next step.                                                                                                                                  | Keeps feedback close to the work. No blocking reward modal, forced animation, or automatic navigation away from the editor.                                                                  |
| Quiet mode           | Hide XP, levels, celebratory feedback, and goal panels; retain ordinary completion, path progress, and next steps. Preserve the preference.                                                                                                                          | Respects users who find game elements distracting. Turning the presentation off does not delete their work.                                                                                  |

The project uses the same practice XP as lessons, without a separate currency or repeat multiplier. Weekly lesson goals exclude the project so their count has a consistent meaning. Hints, explanations, failed attempts, and taking longer never reduce points. Those are normal parts of learning.

## Where it appears

- **Home:** one compact learning-momentum panel after the main introduction. Show practice progress, the optional weekly target, and one next action. Do not replace the curriculum introduction with a scoreboard.
- **Progress:** the complete personal view: XP explanation, milestones with criteria, goal controls, quiet mode, and the existing portable backup. Keep domain progress visible.
- **Beginner guide and paths:** reuse existing completion state. Mark a track milestone inline when its six lessons are checked. Never add a paywall or XP lock to the next lesson.
- **Workspace:** success feedback beside the test result, with an accessible status announcement. The task, editor, and test evidence remain the visual priority.

Keep the black/light-gray foundation. Use existing success, warning, and error tokens for meaning, with text and icons as well as color. Progress bars need accessible names and numeric values. Honor reduced motion. The feature must remain usable at 390 px width, with keyboard navigation and without hover-only explanations.

## State and compatibility contract

The inspected application uses `mastery:learning:v1` and per-item legacy completion keys. Its `updatedAt` changes on page visits and attempts. It is **not a completion date**. Keep these identifiers readable during the ZeroShot rename; a branding change must not erase work.

Recommended implementation boundaries:

1. Derive practice credit from distinct completed current published versions, so reruns cannot inflate XP and two versions never count as two lessons in the score. All current items use version 1. The MVP is a record of currently checked curriculum, not an immutable lifetime score. Define and test a migration policy before publishing version-2 content; preserving historical accomplishments separately from current-version readiness is a later design requirement.
2. Introduce a validated first-completion ledger for new events. Store the lesson ID and version with its local calendar day at the time of completion. Treat existing completions as date-unknown, not as completed today. Do not infer history from `updatedAt` or legacy imports.
3. Credit weekly progress only for known first-completion dates inside the displayed week. Use calendar-date operations, not subtraction of fixed 24-hour durations across daylight-saving boundaries. State the week convention in the UI. Historical local dates should not shift when the learner travels.
4. Validate IDs and dates against the catalog. Malformed, impossible, or future records must not crash rendering or receive current-week credit. A missing catalog entry must not generate a broken quest link.
5. Store the optional goal, quiet-mode preference, and chosen track separately from code drafts. An invalid preference should fall back safely.
6. Export a new backup version if the format gains the ledger/settings. Accept old version-1 Project Mastery backups. Merge progress without replacing existing drafts. Merge a first-completion record idempotently and conservatively; repeated import must not create new credit or celebrations. A legacy record has unknown age and cannot honestly become this week's achievement.
7. Reuse the existing progress event and storage-event subscription for cross-component and cross-tab updates. Read the latest persisted state before mutation. Concurrent completions in two tabs must not erase either accomplishment.
8. If storage is unavailable, explain that the change was not saved. A transient successful run can still show its test result, but must not claim a durable reward was stored.

All of this is personal local progress, not an anti-cheat system. Do not use local XP as a public competitive ranking or call it verified professional competence. A learner can inspect reference code or edit browser storage, and the product should not pretend otherwise.

## Acceptance checks

- A first passing **full suite** grants one lesson's credit. A sample run, page visit, failed suite, duplicate result event, reload, or repeat successful run grants none.
- Legacy completed lessons receive all-time credit and milestone recognition; they do not fabricate a current-week count or trigger a burst of old reward messages.
- Old backups still import; a new export/import round trip preserves progress, first-completion history, preferences, and drafts. Repeated or overlapping imports cannot duplicate XP.
- Multiple versions never count as multiple lessons in the displayed practice score. Historical-credit migration must be designed before a future curriculum version change; this MVP does not claim immutable lifetime credit.
- Week boundaries, year boundaries, daylight-saving changes, and unknown dates are covered by deterministic unit tests. The UI uses the same week definition as the credit calculation.
- Goal changes and quiet mode persist and update other open tabs. A new week resets only that week's target count, never accumulated practice progress.
- All-complete tracks, missing data, corrupt storage, unavailable storage, and catalog growth have useful empty or fallback states. Project links go to project routes rather than lesson routes.
- Browser checks cover first completion, no duplicate credit, settings persistence, legacy migration, backup compatibility, keyboard use, and mobile overflow. Assertions should test the outcome rather than mirror helper implementations.

## Later phases

**Spaced review:** add a distinct review mode that starts from a safe scratch copy, preserving the learner's original solution. Ask for reconstruction or an explanation before revealing a worked solution. A 3-day and 7-day prompt could be an initial heuristic, but tune it using actual delayed performance. Do not award unlimited XP for reopening or rerunning an already filled editor. Distinguish a review completed from a new lesson checked.

**Projects and collaboration:** grow concept-to-project journeys so earlier operations become useful components of a larger system. Later add opt-in peer explanation, small cooperative goals, or shared project showcases when accounts and moderation exist. Do not simulate teammates, endorsements, or community activity in the current local-only product.

**Defer:** global leaderboards, speed bonuses, daily streak loss, limited-time lesson access, currencies, random rewards, paid streak repairs, and mandatory avatar setup. These add pressure or complexity without solving the current problem of helping a learner choose and finish meaningful work.

## Evaluate the design

First run usability sessions: can learners explain how credit is earned, choose an appropriate next step, recover after a break, and turn the game presentation off? Then evaluate return rate alongside delayed reconstruction of an operation and successful transfer to a related task. Include self-reported usefulness and pressure; inspect whether learners switch to easy items solely for counts or avoid challenging projects.

The local-first MVP does not silently add analytics. A later opt-in study can compare the supportive game layer with ordinary progress over several weeks, keeping curriculum and feedback equivalent. More XP or longer sessions alone are not evidence of better learning. Publish measured outcomes before making engagement or retention claims.
